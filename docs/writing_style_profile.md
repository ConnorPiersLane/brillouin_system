# Writing-style profile — Connor Piers Lane

Derived from first-author papers: *Applied Optics* 60(27) 8435 (2021) and 61(6) C37 (2022),
*Optics & Lasers in Engineering* 146 106724 (2021), *Cellulose* 29 6093 (2022),
*Experiments in Fluids* 65:8 (2024), and the ETH doctoral thesis (2022).
Use this profile when drafting or editing anything meant to go out under Connor's name.

---

## Voice and person
- **First-person plural "we"** throughout the body: "We define an error that…", "We demonstrate…",
  "We study the flow…", "We have searched the literature…". This holds **even in sole-authored work** —
  editorial "we" is used for the work and the derivations ("we can furthermore write", "we have used the
  definitions", "we can specify ω").
- **"I" is reserved for authorial/expository choices about the paper itself**, not the science:
  "I will nevertheless also state the relationships for the parameter S₃" (for completeness). Use "we"
  for everything the study does; use "I" only for a deliberate decision about what to include or how to present.
- Never "the authors" in running text.
- The **abstract is third-person/impersonal**: either "This study presents an approach to…" or passive
  "Birefringence measurements … are reported." / "Seven suspensions … are sheared and characterized…".
  The body then switches to active "we".
- **Limitations are stated honestly and early** — often right in the abstract
  ("however, it is not possible to differentiate between fast and slow axis").
- Tone is **plain, direct, engineering-precise** — not literary, not salesy. States what was done and
  what was found. Confidence is earned, never asserted.

## Sentence and paragraph shape
- Short-to-medium **declarative** sentences. One idea per sentence; complex ideas built across a few.
- **Dense multi-sentence paragraphs** with a topic sentence followed by development. No one-line paragraphs.
- Methods are **procedural and concrete**, past tense, with exhaustive instrument detail:
  make, model, supplier, part number, and spec in-line
  (e.g. "a rotatable linear polarizer (Techspec Glass polarizer 50.8 mm; Edmund Optics Inc #66-183;
  extinction ratio 10,000:1)").

## Tense
- **Past tense** for what was done and prepared: "Seven suspensions … were sheared", "The suspensions were
  prepared one week prior…".
- **Present tense** for general truths, model statements, and figure/result description:
  "Figure 7b shows…", "The rods tend to be oriented…", "Δn is a function of the strain rate".

## Structure (IMRaD)
- **Introduction** opens with a general definition of the object of study
  ("Birefringent fluids are composed of optically anisotropic particles…"), funnels through densely-cited
  prior work, and closes with an explicit aim statement — either "In this study, …" or the signature
  **"The aim of this study is to …"**.
- **Methods** in labelled subsections (e.g. "A. Optical Setup", "2.1 Aqueous cellulose nanocrystal suspension").
- **Results and discussion** actively engages the literature: introduces prior studies by name and year
  and reconciles them with the present data ("We have searched the literature for possible further
  explanations and found the subsequent studies particularly helpful. Wayland (1960, 1964) defines…").
  Discrepancies are explained **mechanistically**, not waved away.
- **Conclusion** opens by recapping what was done ("We have presented a simple method to…",
  "In this paper, the Sony … sensor was calibrated…"), states the key findings, and ends with a
  **utility/forward statement that is honestly caveated** — he does not oversell
  ("The use of flow birefringence … is encouraged. However, …").

## Hedging and honesty (a defining trait)
Causal and interpretive claims are deliberately measured. Characteristic hedges:
- "is thought to result from…", "is thought to be…"
- "has to be seen with caution"
- "cannot be excluded", "any … increase does not bring any noticeable improvements"
- "appear relevant in this context", "fairly good agreement"
- "It is important to note that…", "It is, however, important to note that…", "It is striking that…"
This matches his internal project notes: rigorous, self-critical, refuses to claim beyond the data.

## Recurring phrases and tics
- **"with the help of"** — very frequent: "with the help of reference data", "With the help of the Radon
  transform", "with the help of computational analysis".
- **"This is in line with…"** — for agreement with prior work or between results.
- **"Note that…"**, **"In general,"**, **"Therefore,"**, **"Moreover,"**, **"However,"**, **"Similar to …,"**
  as sentence openers.
- **"in the context of"**, **"in the order of"** (uses the British idiom for magnitude: "birefringence
  in the order of 1e-5").
- Figure references: **"Figure 7b shows…"**, **"as depicted in Fig. 1"**, **"(compare Fig. 1)"**, **"see Fig. 6d"**.
- Sets up a caveat with signposting: "Before interpreting these findings, we would like to point out one
  important aspect."

## Mechanics and conventions
- Spelling skews international/British: **"characterized"/"utilized"** (z), but **"orientated"**,
  **"in the order of"**. Keep consistent within a document; match the target journal's house style
  (Optica → US spelling).
- Units: SI, spaced, with reciprocal notation "1/s", "wt%", numeric ranges "between 0.7 and 1.3%".
- Equations numbered and referenced inline; defines every symbol immediately after first use.
- Citations dense in the introduction; author–year (Springer/Elsevier) or numbered (Optica) per venue.

## One-line summary
Plain, precise, "we"-driven engineering prose; exhaustive concrete methods; literature-engaged discussion;
claims that are always honestly hedged and never oversold.
